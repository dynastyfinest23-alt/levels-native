**7. Permanent identity / single-assessment Flow — no findings; reasoning follows since this was the priority check.**

The line I applied: identity content is allowed when it sits inside a first-person option the *user selects to describe their own felt experience*, and disallowed when the *app's voice* (titles, prompts, or any option phrased as a verdict about the user) asserts or implies permanence. Three tests: (a) grammatical person — is it "I/me" or "you"?; (b) epistemic frame — is it hedged as felt experience ("feels like", "a sense that") or stated as fact?; (c) who is concluding — is the user recognizing their own story, or is the app diagnosing?

Applying that:

- "It stopped being a habit a long time ago — now it **feels like** who I am" (`identityFusion`) — first person, hedged with "feels like", and the entire diagnostic point of this option is that the user experiences fusion. This is the user naming their own experience. **Allowed.**
- "Underneath it is a plain **sense** that I am not enough, and never was" (`worthinessGap`) — the harshest line in the file, and the closest call. "And never was" reads as permanent — but it is framed as "a plain sense that", i.e., the content of a felt belief, not a fact the app endorses. A user with a worthiness wound will recognize exactly this sentence; softening it would blunt the option's diagnostic accuracy. First person, hedged, user-selected. **Allowed.**
- "I keep doing it because stopping would mean I am not who I thought I was" (`identityContinuity`) — first person, conditional ("would mean"), describes the mechanism rather than asserting the identity. **Allowed.**
- "...not built for people like me" (`ideologicalSystemic`) — "people like me" is the user's own framing of a worldview the drill exists to surface. **Allowed.**
- App-voice surfaces (titles and prompts) stay clean: "Where it started", "Think about the pattern you keep running into", "How you cope with it" — present-tense, behavioral, no permanence claims, no zone identity. "The pattern you keep running into" asserts recurrence, which is the drill's premise, not a permanence verdict.

No option is phrased as the app telling the user who they are. Pass.

**8. Variable reward framing — no findings.** Nothing implies scores are variable, negotiable, or inflated. Pass (largely N/A to static drill copy).

**9. AI-tell vocabulary / puffery — no banned words; two borderline triples noted.** No `delete-ai-words` violations (no unlock/elevate/journey/seamless/empower/dive-in/game-changer), no inflated claims. Two labels use triple-parallel structure, which the rubric lists as "rule-of-three padding":

- "I shut down — **go quiet, go flat, wait it out**" (Q3, `collapseShutdown`)
- "**rigged, unfair, not built for people like me**" (Q2, `ideologicalSystemic`)

I judge both non-blocking: they read as natural compressed speech, and each is concrete rather than puffery. But in both, the first two items are near-synonyms, so tightening is free (fixes below). A third list — "bosses, parents, rules" (Q2, `internalizedAuthority`) — is three concrete examples, not rhetorical padding; no change needed.

**10. Reframe rhetoric — no findings; reasoning follows.** Several options use contrast structures: "Something I was taught to believe, never something I decided for myself"; "always getting ready, never actually starting"; "I keep the idea of what I could do alive, and never actually test it". These are structurally adjacent to "not X, it's Y" but I judge them pass: the contrast *is the diagnostic content* (inherited vs. chosen; preparing vs. starting) spoken in the user's own voice — not the app's voice performing a rhetorical pivot on the user. Criterion 10 targets style-as-rhetoric in the app's narration; none of the titles or prompts do this. Pass.

## Other quality findings (beyond the 10 criteria)

**A. Register mismatch in Q1 — two options answer the wrong question (worth fixing).** Q1 asks "where does it actually come from?" and ten of twelve options answer with an origin story (something that happened, was taught, was modeled, was absorbed). Two answer with *present-tense behavior*, which is Q3's register, not an origin:

- "I stay in preparation mode — always getting ready, never actually starting" (`preparationLoop`)
- "I keep every option open on purpose, so nothing is ever fully decided" (`optionalityPreservation`)

A user tracing their pattern back will find these two options describing the pattern itself rather than its source, which weakens the discrimination between Q1 and Q3. Suggested origin-flavored rewrites:

- `preparationLoop`: "At some point, getting ready became safer than being judged on the result — and I never left that mode"
- `optionalityPreservation`: "Somewhere along the way, keeping every option open started to feel safer than committing to one"

(`worthinessGap` also answers with a present state, but "Underneath it is..." explicitly frames it as the root beneath the pattern, so it earns its place in Q1.)

**B. Cross-question semantic overlap (note only, no fix required).** Q3's `preservedPotential` ("I keep the idea of what I could do alive, and never actually test it") covers nearly the same ground as Q1's `preparationLoop`/`optionalityPreservation`. If fix A above is taken, the overlap shrinks (Q1 becomes about where it started, Q3 stays about what the user does), so I recommend fix A rather than touching Q3. Similarly, Q1 `identityFusion` and Q3 `identityContinuity` share a construct but from origin vs. coping angles, which is legitimate.

**C. "Actually" tic (note only).** "Actually" appears six times across the file: Q1 prompt, Q2 prompt, Q3 prompt, Q3 free-text prompt, and two option labels. In the three prompts it is doing deliberate work (cutting past the user's rehearsed answer to the real one), and I would keep it there. But four-of-five prompts plus two options is a detectable house tic. Cheapest trim: drop it from the Q3 free-text prompt — "Say more about how you handle it, in your own words." — which also makes the two free-text prompts parallel.

**D. Emotion-label register (note only).** The file's own doc comment says options are "behavioral descriptions, never emotion labels", but two Q2 options lead with felt states: "A quiet fear of being found out..." and "Big-picture dread — the sense something is fundamentally wrong...". For a *domain* question ("where does it show up"), a purely behavioral phrasing is awkward — the domain sometimes just is an internal felt pattern — so I judge these acceptable, but noting the tension with the file's stated convention in case Noah wants Q2 held to the same standard as the Phase 1 assessment options.
